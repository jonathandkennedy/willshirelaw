Do the Math

Wage Theft Is Bigger Than Every Other Kind of Theft Combined

A few dollars an hour doesn't sound like much. Multiply it by every shift, every week, for three years — then add the penalties California stacks on top.

It goes back three years

California lets you recover unpaid wages going back three years — four under the Unfair Competition Law. That "small" shortfall on every check is a very large number by now.

Penalties stack fast

One extra hour of pay for every missed meal break. Another for every missed rest break. Up to 30 days' wages if your final check was late. Penalties for inaccurate pay stubs. It adds up faster than the wages themselves.

It's illegal to fire you for asking

Retaliation for a wage complaint is a separate violation with its own damages. You're protected — and if they punish you for calling us, your case just got bigger.

What You Could Recover

Every Violation Has a Price Tag in California

You don't have to prove they were mean. You just have to prove they didn't pay. The Labor Code does the rest.

Unpaid overtime

Time-and-a-half after 8 hours in a day or 40 in a week — double time after 12. "Salaried" doesn't automatically mean exempt, and "we don't pay overtime here" isn't a policy, it's a violation.

Meal & rest break premiums

One extra hour of pay for each missed, short, or late meal break — and another hour for each missed rest break. Per day. Going back years.

Off-the-clock work

Pre-shift setup, post-shift cleanup, answering texts at home, bag checks, "just finish this before you clock in" — all compensable. All owed.

Misclassification

Called a contractor but treated like an employee? Called "exempt" but doing hourly work? You may be owed overtime, breaks, expenses, and benefits for the entire period.

Waiting-time & pay-stub penalties

Late or short final paycheck? Up to 30 days of wages as a penalty. Inaccurate pay stubs? Penalties per pay period. Plus interest, plus your attorney's fees — paid by them.

Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

I'm salaried. Can I still get overtime?

Often, yes. A salary alone doesn't make you "exempt." To be exempt in California you generally must earn at least twice the state minimum wage for full-time work AND spend most of your time on genuinely managerial, administrative, or professional duties. Assistant managers who mostly run a register, "coordinators" doing hourly work — these are classic misclassification cases.

I signed something saying I'm an independent contractor.

Doesn't matter. California's ABC test looks at how you actually work — who controls your hours, whether your work is the company's core business, whether you truly run your own business. A label on a form can't sign away your rights.

Can they fire me for filing a wage claim?

Retaliating against you for asserting your wage rights is illegal and creates a second, separate claim with its own damages. Everything you tell us is confidential, and your employer will not hear from us until you decide you're ready.

How long do I have to file?

Deadlines are shorter than people think. Discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims under California's Fair Employment and Housing Act must be filed with the Civil Rights Department within three years. Wrongful termination in violation of public policy generally carries a two-year deadline. Unpaid-wage claims reach back three years (up to four under the Unfair Competition Law). Some notice deadlines are far shorter — so get a free review now, not after the deadline passes.

My coworkers are getting shorted the same way. Does that matter?

It matters a lot. When a policy shorts everyone, we can bring a class action or a PAGA (Private Attorneys General Act) claim — which changes the leverage completely. Our wage-and-hour team has taken class and PAGA actions to verdict.

What does it cost?

Nothing up front — ever. We front all costs, we only collect a fee if we recover money for you, and California law generally makes the employer pay your attorney's fees when you win.
