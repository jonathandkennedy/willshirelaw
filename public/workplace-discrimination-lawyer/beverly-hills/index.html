Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

What's protected under California law?

Race, color, national origin, ancestry, religion, age (40+), sex, gender, gender identity and expression, sexual orientation, pregnancy and related conditions, disability and medical conditions, genetic information, marital status, military or veteran status, and requesting or taking protected leave. Being punished for complaining about any of it is retaliation — also illegal.

They never said anything discriminatory out loud. Can I still prove it?

Yes. Most discrimination cases are proven by pattern: timing, comparisons to how others were treated, shifting explanations, and who replaced you. That's the work we do.

I asked for an accommodation and things went downhill. Is that a case?

Often, yes. California requires employers to engage in a good-faith "interactive process" and provide reasonable accommodations for disabilities, medical conditions, and pregnancy. Refusing, stalling, or punishing you for asking is actionable.

How long do I have to file?

Deadlines are shorter than people think. Discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims under California's Fair Employment and Housing Act must be filed with the Civil Rights Department within three years. Wrongful termination in violation of public policy generally carries a two-year deadline. Unpaid-wage claims reach back three years (up to four under the Unfair Competition Law). Some notice deadlines are far shorter — so get a free review now, not after the deadline passes.

I still work there. Can I really do this?

Yes. Retaliation for asserting your rights is illegal and creates a second claim. Everything you tell us is confidential, and your employer won't hear from us until you decide.

What does it cost?

Nothing up front — ever. We front all costs, we only collect a fee if we recover money for you, and FEHA generally makes the employer pay your attorney's fees when you win.
