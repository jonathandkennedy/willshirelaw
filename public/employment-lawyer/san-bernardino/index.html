Not "maybe." Not "if they feel like it." These are the damages the law puts on the table when an employer breaks it.

Lost wages & benefits

Back pay from the day it happened, plus front pay for the income you'll lose while you rebuild — health coverage, bonuses, and retirement contributions included.

Emotional distress

Anxiety, humiliation, sleepless nights — California's Fair Employment and Housing Act lets a jury put a dollar figure on what they did to you.

Penalties that stack

Late final paycheck? Up to 30 days of wages in waiting-time penalties. Missed meal or rest breaks? An extra hour of pay for each one — for every shift, for years.

Punitive damages

When an employer acts with malice or fraud, juries can award punitive damages designed to make sure they never do it again.

Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

How much does an employment lawyer cost?

Nothing up front — ever. The consultation is free, we front the costs of building your case, and we only collect a fee if we recover money for you. Many California employment laws also make the employer pay your attorney's fees when you win. No recovery, no fee.

I was an "at-will" employee. Can I still have a case?

Yes. At-will means they can fire you for no reason — not for an illegal reason. Firing you because of your age, race, gender, pregnancy, disability, medical leave, or because you complained about something illegal is still wrongful termination. Let us look before you assume you have nothing.

They offered me severance. Should I sign it?

Not before a free review. Severance agreements almost always include a release of every claim you have — and employers rarely offer severance unless they're worried about something. A 60-second call can tell you whether that offer is a fair deal or a fraction of what you're owed.

How long do I have to file?

Deadlines are shorter than people think. Discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims under California's Fair Employment and Housing Act must be filed with the Civil Rights Department within three years. Wrongful termination in violation of public policy generally carries a two-year deadline. Unpaid-wage claims reach back three years (up to four under the Unfair Competition Law). Some notice deadlines are far shorter — so get a free review now, not after the deadline passes.

Will I have to go to court?

Most cases settle without a trial. But employers track which firms actually try cases — and pay more to avoid facing them. Our employment team has taken wage-and-hour class actions to verdict. When they know you'll go the distance, they settle.

Can my employer retaliate against me for calling a lawyer?

Retaliating against you for asserting your rights is itself illegal — and it becomes a second claim on top of the first. Everything you tell us is confidential. Your employer will not hear from us until you decide you want them to.
