Why Truck Cases Are Different

The Trucking Company Started Building Its Defense at the Scene

Commercial carriers carry seven- and eight-figure policies — and they have rapid-response teams whose only job is to protect them. Here's what they're doing right now.

Evidence has a shelf life

The truck's electronic logging device, dashcam, GPS, and black box data can be overwritten in days. Federal rules only require carriers to keep some records for months. A preservation demand from us the day you hire us is the difference.

Multiple defendants, multiple policies

The driver. The carrier. The company that loaded the trailer. The broker. The maintenance shop. Each one may carry its own coverage — and each one will try to blame the others. We pursue all of them.

They will try to blame you

"He cut me off." "She was in my blind spot." The carrier's investigators arrive with a story already written. Ours arrive with the federal safety rules the driver was supposed to follow.

What You Could Recover

Truck Crashes Cause Catastrophic Injuries. The Law Prices Them Accordingly.

An 80,000-pound truck against a 4,000-pound car doesn't produce fender-benders. Here's what a full recovery includes.

Lifetime medical care

Surgeries, rehabilitation, spinal and brain injury treatment, home care, mobility equipment — priced for the rest of your life by medical and economic experts, not by the adjuster.

Lost income & earning capacity

If you can't return to your work — or any work — the value of every paycheck you would have earned is part of the case.

Pain and suffering

The physical pain, the trauma, the life you had before. California places no cap on these damages in most injury cases, and juries in truck cases award accordingly.

Wrongful death damages

If you lost someone, the family can recover funeral costs, lost financial support, and the loss of love, companionship, and guidance.

Punitive damages

Falsified logbooks, skipped inspections, drivers pushed past federal hour limits — when a carrier's misconduct is willful, juries can add punitive damages to punish it.

Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

Why do I need a truck accident lawyer specifically?

Because truck cases are governed by federal safety regulations, involve multiple potential defendants and policies, and hinge on electronic evidence that disappears fast. Carriers have specialized defense teams. You need a firm that has taken a truck case to a $35 million verdict.

The trucking company's insurer called and wants a statement.

Don't give one. You're not required to, and every word will be used to shrink your claim. Politely tell them your lawyer will be in touch — then call us.

How much is my truck accident case worth?

Truck cases are typically worth far more than car cases because the injuries are more severe and the policies are larger. It depends on your injuries, your prognosis, your income, and how badly the carrier behaved. A free review gives you a realistic range.

How long do I have to file?

In California you generally have two years from the accident to file a lawsuit (Code of Civil Procedure §335.1) — and if a city, county, or state agency is involved (a bus, a public road, a government vehicle) you may have as little as six months to file a claim. Evidence disappears far sooner: camera footage gets erased and witnesses move. The sooner we start, the stronger your case.

What does it cost?

Nothing up front — ever. Truck cases are expensive to build — reconstruction experts, trucking-industry experts, medical experts. We front all of it, and we only collect a fee if we recover money for you.

What if I was partly at fault?

You can still recover. California uses pure comparative negligence — your recovery is reduced by your share of fault, even if it's more than half. Don't accept the carrier's version of who's to blame.
