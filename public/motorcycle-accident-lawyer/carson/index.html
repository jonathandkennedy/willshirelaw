Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

I was lane splitting. Do I still have a case?

Yes. Lane splitting is legal in California. The question is whether you were doing it reasonably — and whether the driver who hit you was paying attention. Insurers use lane splitting to scare riders out of claims. Don't let them.

I wasn't wearing a helmet.

You may still recover. California's comparative negligence rules mean your recovery could be reduced for injuries a helmet would have prevented — but the driver who caused the crash is still responsible for the crash. Get a free review before you assume anything.

The insurance company already offered me money. Should I take it?

Not before a free case review. First offers to riders are especially low because insurers assume you'll take it. Signing usually ends your claim permanently, even if your injuries get worse.

How long do I have to file?

In California you generally have two years from the accident to file a lawsuit (Code of Civil Procedure §335.1) — and if a city, county, or state agency is involved (a bus, a public road, a government vehicle) you may have as little as six months to file a claim. Evidence disappears far sooner: camera footage gets erased and witnesses move. The sooner we start, the stronger your case.

I don't have health insurance. Can I still get treated?

Yes. We help clients get the care they need — often on a lien, so providers are paid from the settlement instead of out of your pocket. Don't skip treatment; it hurts your health and your case.

What does it cost?

Nothing up front — ever. We front every cost of building your case and only collect a fee if we recover money for you. No recovery, no attorney fee.
