What You Could Recover

Wrongful Termination Isn't Just "Your Old Paycheck"

People think a firing case is worth a few weeks of wages. Here's what the law actually puts on the table.

Back pay

Every dollar you would have earned from the day they fired you until your case resolves — salary, overtime, commissions, bonuses, and the benefits that came with them.

Front pay

The income you'll keep losing while you find comparable work. For senior roles or specialized careers, this is often the biggest number in the case.

Emotional distress

Being fired illegally is humiliating and terrifying. Juries in California regularly award significant damages for what it did to your health, your family, and your peace of mind.

Punitive damages

When a company fires you with malice — to punish you for speaking up, or to dodge a disability accommodation — the jury can award punitive damages meant to hurt.

Attorney's fees, paid by them

Under the Fair Employment and Housing Act and other California laws, the employer pays your attorney's fees when you win. Which is exactly why we charge you nothing up front.

Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

What counts as wrongful termination in California?

Being fired for an illegal reason — because of a protected characteristic (age 40+, race, gender, pregnancy, disability, religion, sexual orientation), in retaliation for complaining about harassment, discrimination, unpaid wages or safety violations, for taking protected medical or family leave, for refusing to do something illegal, or in breach of a contract. If the timing feels suspicious, it probably is.

They gave me a reason. Doesn't that end it?

No. Employers almost always give a reason — "performance," "restructuring," "budget." The question is whether it's the real reason. Years of good reviews followed by a sudden write-up after you complained is exactly what juries see through. We know how to prove pretext.

Should I sign the severance agreement?

Not before a free review. Severance agreements release every claim you have. Once we know what your case is worth, we can often negotiate a far larger severance — or turn a small offer into a real settlement.

How long do I have to file?

Deadlines are shorter than people think. Discrimination, harassment, and retaliation claims under California's Fair Employment and Housing Act must be filed with the Civil Rights Department within three years. Wrongful termination in violation of public policy generally carries a two-year deadline. Unpaid-wage claims reach back three years (up to four under the Unfair Competition Law). Some notice deadlines are far shorter — so get a free review now, not after the deadline passes.

How much is my wrongful termination case worth?

It depends on your pay, how long you were there, how hard it is to replace your job, and how badly they behaved. Cases range from a few months' wages to seven figures. A free case review gives you a realistic range before you sign anything.

Can I still file for unemployment?

Yes — and you should. Filing for unemployment does not hurt your wrongful termination case, and if the employer contests it, what they say to the EDD often becomes evidence for us.
