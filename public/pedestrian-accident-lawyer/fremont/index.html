Pedestrian and cyclist injuries are among the most severe on the road — and insurers fight them the hardest. Here's what they're doing right now.

"They came out of nowhere"

Every pedestrian case starts with the driver claiming you darted out, were on your phone, or weren't in the crosswalk. Footage, witnesses, and reconstruction settle that argument — if someone preserves them fast.

Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

I wasn't in a crosswalk. Do I still have a case?

Very likely. Drivers have a duty to watch for pedestrians everywhere, and California's comparative negligence rules mean you can recover even if you share some fault — your recovery is just reduced by your percentage. Don't let the insurer talk you out of a claim.

It was a hit-and-run. Who pays?

Possibly your own auto policy's uninsured-motorist coverage (even though you were on foot), a family member's policy, or — if the driver is later identified — theirs. We investigate every avenue.

The driver's insurer already offered me money.

Don't sign. Pedestrian injuries often get worse before they get better, and a signed release ends your claim permanently. A free review takes 60 seconds.

How long do I have to file?

In California you generally have two years from the accident to file a lawsuit (Code of Civil Procedure §335.1) — and if a city, county, or state agency is involved (a bus, a public road, a government vehicle) you may have as little as six months to file a claim. Evidence disappears far sooner: camera footage gets erased and witnesses move. The sooner we start, the stronger your case.

I don't have health insurance. Can I still get treated?

Yes. We help clients get the care they need — often on a lien, so providers are paid from the settlement instead of out of your pocket. Don't skip treatment.

What does it cost?

Nothing up front — ever. We front every cost of building your case and only collect a fee if we recover money for you. No recovery, no attorney fee.
