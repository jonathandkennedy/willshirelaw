What Your Family Can Recover

The Law Cannot Undo It. It Can Make Sure Your Family Is Provided For.

These are the damages California law makes available to the family of someone killed by negligence.

Lost financial support

The income, benefits, and household contributions your loved one would have provided over a lifetime — calculated by economic experts, not by an adjuster.

Funeral & medical expenses

Burial costs and any medical bills from the final injury or illness.

Loss of love, companionship & guidance

California allows families to recover for the relationship itself — the care, comfort, protection, and guidance that was taken from a spouse, a child, a parent.

The survival claim

Through the estate, the pain and suffering your loved one endured before passing, and — where the conduct was reckless — punitive damages to punish it.

Accountability

A trucking company that pushed a driver past his limits. A property owner who ignored a hazard for months. A verdict says out loud what happened — and makes it less likely to happen to the next family.

Straight Answers

Questions Everyone Asks

Who can file a wrongful death claim in California?

Generally the surviving spouse or domestic partner, children, and — if there are none — other heirs such as parents or siblings. People who were financially dependent on the deceased, including stepchildren and parents, may also qualify. We'll walk you through who in your family can bring the claim.

Is it too soon to call?

It's never too soon to have someone protect your family's rights — and it costs nothing. You don't have to decide anything today. But evidence disappears and deadlines run, and we can quietly preserve both while you grieve.

The insurance company already reached out to us.

Please don't sign anything or give a recorded statement. Let us handle every call from here. Early offers in death cases are made precisely because families are in no state to evaluate them.

How long do we have to file?

In California you generally have two years from the accident to file a lawsuit (Code of Civil Procedure §335.1) — and if a city, county, or state agency is involved (a bus, a public road, a government vehicle) you may have as little as six months to file a claim. Evidence disappears far sooner: camera footage gets erased and witnesses move. The sooner we start, the stronger your case.

Will we have to go to court?

Most cases settle. But the other side pays more when they know the family's lawyers will try the case — and we do. If we go to trial, we prepare you and stand with you every step.

What does it cost?

Nothing up front — ever. We front every cost of building the case and only collect a fee if we recover for your family. No recovery, no fee.
